Weaving — Chikage Imai

Bio: Chikage Imai http://www.chikageimai.net Studying under artists like Yuasa Joji, Matsui Akihiko, Wim Henderickx and Fabio Nieder, Imai graduated with a degree in Composition from the Aichi University of the Arts in 2002 and received her master’s degree at the Conservatorium van Amsterdam in 2009. Her works have been commissioned by variable organizations and musicians from all over the world such as Festival d’Automne à Paris, Westdeutscher Rundfunk, Music from Japan, Hanatsu~miroir, Vertixe Sonora Ensemble, Tosiya Suzuki and so on.

In her compositions, Imai regards music as an organism and explores her concept “vector in music” to expand its sensory possibilities. Her artistic practice has led Imai to engage in and direct various interdisciplinary projects that deepened her connection with visual art, photography, contemporary dance and scenography.  In recent years, Imai’s strong interest in “a close coexistence between art and society” has led her to organize a lecture and concert series entitled “Crossboundary”, as well as the Contemporary Music Academy, to further establish the contemporary music scene in Nagoya. These projects opened up opportunities to participate in locally-rooted projects and events including Nakagawa Canal Project - ARToC10 and Aichi Triennale 2016, 2019 (Open Competition for Stage Performance), among others. In 2020 Imai was offered to write a short piece for a big chamber ensemble piece for the 40th anniversary of Ensemble Modern as only one Japanese composer after master composers like Ennio Morricone and Helmut Lachenmann. Her interdisciplinary work with photography and visual art “Synecdochism III” was created for Aichi Triennale 2022 and received a lot of praise from people who are not only involved in contemporary music but also contemporary art. Moreover, in the winter of 2023, a commissioned work “Starry Bulges for 21 musicians” celebrating the 20th anniversary of the International Ensemble Modern Academy premiered and received high praise. Imai’s music is published Muzikproduktion Dabringhaus und Grimm and Ensemble Modern Media. Through being an honorary research associate at Royal Holloway, University of London, she is currently an artistic director of Seainx project.

Dark Night Above the Woods — Michael Blazek (2025 Finalist)

The piece evokes the atmosphere of a nocturnal forest, its silence, concealed life, and sudden bursts of movement. It unfolds in four movements.

Originally conceived for five instruments, including accordion, the work was later adapted to fit the instrumentation of the HICMF ensemble. In this revised version, the accordion is replaced by strings, and the ensemble is expanded with percussion.

This transformation does more than extend the palette: the addition of percussion intensifies the music’s visceral, animalistic energy, bringing out a wilder and more untamed character. Subtle revisions throughout the piece further sharpen its gestures, making the music more immediate and forceful, reinforcing its dark, raw, and organic sound world.

Bio: Michael Blazek is a Slovak-born composer based in Canada. His music is driven by rhythm, unfolding as raw, untamed energy shaped through a distinctive, microtonally inflected sonic language. His work has been presented across Europe and North America, as well as in South Africa at the ISCM World New Music Days.

 

Sequenza VIIb — Luciano Berio

Sequenza VII (composed 1969) is a composition for solo oboe by Luciano Berio, the seventh of his fourteen Sequenze. “The Sequenzas are a series of works for sixteen different solo instruments, including trombone, violin, voice, piano, guitar and accordion, are some of Berio's most famous works. All share a common virtuosic element and are "intended to set out and to develop melodically an essentially harmonic discourse and... a polyphonic mode of listening." Sequenza VII was originally written in 1969 for the oboist Heinz Holliger and was transcribed for soprano saxophone by the composer and Claude Delangle in 1993. Berio has come to prefer this version and had planned to rewrite the oboe version with the effects of the soprano saxophone in mind.

Sequenza VIIb could be perceived as a variation on one note, B natural. This pitch sounds quietly through the whole piece from another instrument or synthesizer in order to "lend a weak echo to the solo instrument." From the starting pitch, B, an almost complete chromatic register is gradually introduced. The backbone of the work lies in enharmonic fingerings, multiphonics, articulation, and dynamics. The work can create a truly electrophonic impression.

Bio: Luciano Berio taught at Mills College, Harvard University, and the Juilliard School. From 1973 to 1980 he headed the electroacoustic department of IRCAM in Paris. In 1960 and 1982 he was composer in residence at the Tanglewood Music Center, and in 1993-94 delivered the Norton Lectures at Harvard University. One of the most prolific and influential composers of the late twentieth century, Berio received many commissions, prizes, and honorary degrees, and composed for voice, symphony orchestra, music theater, and solo instruments, as well as exploring electronic and digital processing of sound. He also transcribed and adapted works by composers ranging from Monteverdi to Mahler, and collaborated with major Italian literary figures including Italo Calvino and Umberto Eco.” (source). Berio passed away in 2003.

 

 

 

 

 

 

Two Tigers — David Jaeger

This work is a short set of double variations. The theme is made up of 2 phrases, one chromatic and one diatonic. The variations are alternatively based on these contrasting phrases. This process parallels an image by the late Toronto artist, Milton Jewell. In Jewell's painting, two tigers are resting on a proscenium, their gazes distant and self-absorbed, as if lost in reflection. In musical terms, I think of each variation as a depiction of the private musings of the two creatures, which often turn out to be less than ferocious. 

Bio: David Jaeger

 

The Machine, The Heart — Boya Liu (Call for Scores Finalist)

“The Machine, the Heart” explores the complex and multilayered relationship between the mechanical and the human heart. The machine is often perceived as a symbol of coldness, precision, and detachment, while the “heart” represents warmth, emotion, and life. Yet in the contemporary context, this boundary has become increasingly blurred: machines can respond to humans with patience and gentleness, while collective human behavior often reveals a programmed, unconscious indifference.

The work does not portray a binary opposition or a transformation between the two; instead, it seeks meaning in their coexistence. Warmth is concealed within mechanical order, and traces of structure and algorithm are embedded within emotional flow. The clarinet and string quartet mirror one another and exchange identities—humanized sonorities may emerge from the “mechanical,” while gentle resonance may arise from the “programmed.” At the core of the music lies the conjunction “and”: the machine and the heart are not separated poles, but mutually dependent, co-breathing entities.

Within this framework, the work positions two extremes in its notation practice: the precise, New-Complexity-inspired writing and the non-metrical, improvisatory notation, symbolizing radical order and radical disorder. At the same time, the clarinet and string quartet form another field of contrasting yet interdependent energies: one embodying mechanical and systematic forces, the other pointing toward the organic, the breathing, and the living. Their interaction does not aim for opposition, but allows these seemingly conflicting forces to penetrate and coexist within the sound.

Bio: Boya Liu (b. 1999, Tangshan, China) is a composer, clarinetist, and music theorist. He completed his undergraduate studies in clarinet performance at the Tianjin Conservatory of Music in 2021, where he also began studying composition and music theory. During his studies, he performed with the Tianjin Symphony Orchestra under the baton of Muhai Tang.

He is currently pursuing a master’s degree in Music Theory and Composition at the Central Conservatory of Music in Beijing, studying counterpoint with Professor Fayong Liang. From 2024 to 2025, he continued his composition studies at the Royal Conservatoire of Scotland with Bekah Simms.

His compositional work focuses on the exploration of timbre and texture as primary structural elements. Rather than relying on traditional melodic or harmonic development, his music often develops through the transformation, layering, and interaction of sonic materials, with particular attention to density, resonance, and the perceptual shaping of sound.

Liu’s works have been performed in China and internationally by ensembles including the Beijing Modern Chamber Ensemble and Ensemble L’Itinéraire. His music has received awards in a number of national and international competitions, including Second Prize in the Toyama International Contemporary Music Festival Opening Composition Competition (Japan), recognition in the 11th ConTempo Composition Competition, and awards from the 20th SunRiver New Music Competition, among others.

In addition to his compositional practice, Liu has presented research on 20th-century polyphony at international academic conferences, reflecting an ongoing engagement with both creative and theoretical approaches to contemporary music.

 

Turbulence — Tianci Lu (Call for Scores Finalist)

Turbulence captures the feeling of being surrounded by a powerful force of nature. It can be unpredictable, but at times also beautiful and breathtaking. It explores the various sonorities of the ensemble, and uses each instrument to function not only as a melodic instrument, but also as a conduit for creating texture and ambiance, with the introduction of the piece establishing a sonic wind-like movement.

Members of the ensemble performing this piece must be sensitive to the group sound, and how each instrument’s part interacts and blends to create the desired effect of atmospheric turbulence.

The piece frequently fluctuates between moments of intense chaos and moments of peaceful serenity. Throughout the piece, some parts intentionally blur or stretch the rhythm, contributing to an overarching sense of unpredictability. Given the nature of turbulence, expect the piece to evolve in dynamic “waves”, where quiet, calm sections might gradually swell into louder, more intense figures. Turbulence evokes the mayhem of being caught up in a storm, and will sweep the listeners away on a journey through the whirlwind.

Bio: Tianci Lu’s musical journey began as a classical pianist, then as a composer. Her forays into the music profession truly began upon her graduation from the Shanghai Conservatory of Music in 2020, where she had double major in both composition and composition for screen. Seeking to learn more about developing a stronger understanding of jazz to supplement her own works, Tianci then earned a Master’s in Jazz piano at the California Institute of the Arts. Tianci currently is pursuing her doctoral degree in music composition at Princeton University. Tianci has immense versatility in genres, and utilizes her skillsets in a wide variety of styles to creatively construct creative scores and melodies from the stories and feelings that she wishes to sonically portray. She loves to combine multiple genres of music in synchronous ways, fusing ideas from separate parts to yield cohesive results.

 

A Vertical Dream — Mehrdad Jafari Raad (Call for Scores Finalist)

What kind of world does the mind build while we sleep? Dreaming is experience, not a remembered story or a symbolic code. It’s a state in which we feel ourselves present in a world, complete with sensory presence, spatial depth, and lived immersion.

A Vertical Dream comes from my interest in this world-like quality. For me, dreams aren’t puzzles to interpret, but shifting spaces, unstable and continuously transforming with no clear resolution.

Within my dreams, horizons feel limiting. Flat openness closes down the imagination. What pulls me forward is verticality, of moving through spaces that hide as much as they reveal. This impulse shapes the music. Drones establish a ground, while repeating ascending figures create lift and tension. Solo gestures surface briefly, then dissolve back into the texture.

This is not music about a dream. It is an invitation to step inside one, and to listen as its space slowly takes form.

Bio: Mehrdad Jafari Raad is a Toronto-based composer, performer, and media artist whose work spans concert music, film scoring, and electronic and electroacoustic practices. He holds a Doctor of Musical Arts in Composition from the University of Toronto. His music draws on traditional Iranian music alongside other musical traditions encountered through his studies, engaging contemporary classical idioms through a nuanced, narrative-driven approach.

His concert works have been performed internationally and have received numerous awards, and he has collaborated with a wide range of ensembles and performers, including the Bedford Trio, Rogue Duo, and the Brooklyn Chamber Orchestra. Alongside his concert practice, his film scores have been featured at major international festivals such as TIFF, VIFF, and San Sebastián.

In parallel with his work for the concert hall and screen, he collaborates with visual artists on interdisciplinary projects that explore multimodal forms and hybrid performance practices. His current work focuses on creating dialogues between sound and image, structure and improvisation. Across his practice, he engages themes of identity and fragmentation, cultural synthesis, power and resistance, and inner psychological landscapes.

Stephen Morris, HICMF Co-Producer

Stephen Morris is a North Carolina–based composer whose music explores atmospheric, immersive, and trance-like sound worlds. His works have been performed internationally at venues and institutions including Carnegie Hall, Arizona State University, New York University, Seoul National University, the Royal Conservatory of Music, Chateau de Fontainebleau, Trojhalí Karolina, New Holland Island, and the University of North Carolina Greensboro.

Morris has collaborated with a wide range of ensembles and artists, including the Prague Philharmonia, Imani Winds, Moscow Contemporary Ensemble, Hanzhi Wang, Studio for New Music Ensemble, Li-Chin Li, Gemini Duo, Hypercube, the Brooklyn Chamber Orchestra, and the Lincoln Center Theater Directors Lab. His music has also been featured at festivals and institutes such as Ostrava Days, Écoles d’art américaines de Fontainebleau, Impuls, reMusik, Don’t Sleep in Yancheng, the Scarborough Philharmonic Festival, the Imani Winds Chamber Music Festival, Charlotte New Music Festival, Music at the Close, and the SCI Regional Conference.

A winner of the Karen Kieser Prize in Canadian Music, Morris has studied with Huang Ruo and Reiko Füting, and has participated in lessons and master classes with leading composers including Tristan Murail, George Lewis, Christian Wolff, Bernhard Lang, Bright Sheng, Ana Sokolovic, and Zosha Di Castri. He holds a Doctor of Musical Arts degree from the University of Toronto and a Master of Music degree from Mannes School of Music. He currently serves on the composition faculty at the University of North Carolina Charlotte.

See: https://www.stephenmorrismusic.org/

 

David Jaeger, C.M., HICMF Artistic Advisor

David Jaeger is a music producer, composer and broadcaster, who created "Two New Hours", an important contemporary music series in Canada which was heard on the national CBC Radio Two network from 1978 to 2007. During that time he commissioned over 250 original musical works for presentation on air, and broadcast more that 3,000 world premiere performances.

From 1974 to 2002 he served as the CBC Radio coordinator of the CBC/Radio-Canada National Radio Competition for Young Composers, encouraging the establishment of a new wave of Canadian composers.

In 2002 Jaeger was elected President of the International Rostrum of Composers, and was the only non-European ever to be named to this post. He served as President for six years.

Jaeger's compositions range widely from purely melodic unaccompanied works to chamber music, orchestral works and electronic and mixed media compositions. He is a founding member of the Canadian Electronic Ensemble, a live electronic group that remains active.

David Jaeger was named a member of the Order of Canada in 2018.

See https://davidjaeger.ca

 

Bob Becker, HICMF Musical Advisor

Bob Becker is a percussionist, composer, arranger, and founding member of the acclaimed percussion ensemble NEXUS. A graduate of the Eastman School of Music, where he earned bachelor’s and master’s degrees and the Performer’s Certificate, he later pursued postgraduate study in world music at Wesleyan University, focusing on the music of India, Africa, and Indonesia. Becker’s career spans orchestral, chamber, contemporary, world, and solo percussion traditions. He has performed with the Marlboro Festival Orchestra under Pablo Casals, the Paul Winter Consort, Tafelmusik Baroque Orchestra, Ensemble Intercontemporaine, Ensemble Modern, and many major symphony orchestras. Since 1973, he has also been a member of Steve Reich and Musicians, contributing to landmark performances and recordings, including the Grammy-winning Music for 18 Musicians.

Internationally recognized as a leading xylophone and marimba virtuoso, Becker has helped revive early 20th-century xylophone repertoire and performance practice. His work with NEXUS has taken him around the world and earned honors including induction into the Percussive Arts Society Hall of Fame. He has studied and performed extensively in West African and North Indian musical traditions, including tabla study with Pandit Sharda Sahai.

As a composer, Becker’s works and arrangements are performed worldwide and published by Keyboard Percussion Publications. His compositions include There is a Time, Music On The Moon, Turning Point, Preludes, and Water Lilies. He has also collaborated with dance companies, lectured internationally, designed instruments and mallets with Malletech and Sabian, and authored Rudimental Arithmetic, a study of snare drum rudiments, composition, and polyrhythmic theory.

See: https://www.nexuspercussion.com/members/bob-becker-bio/
